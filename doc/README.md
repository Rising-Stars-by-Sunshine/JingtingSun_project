## Field Trip Reflection — AI Products: AWS vs. Tencent  

### Part A — AWS vs. Tencent (observations, promise & peril)
On site, the **AWS** briefing emphasized modular, audit-ready cloud ML: **SageMaker** for training/hosting, **Bedrock** for foundation models, data lakes, and compliance tooling (IAM, KMS, CloudTrail). In the **Tencent** exhibition hall we saw city-scale dashboards and healthcare demos, with a clear **Green Cloud** theme: carbon-aware scheduling, renewables in data centers, and **PUE/WUE** tracking.

**Promise.** Cloud ML surfaces patterns quickly—triage in hospitals, traffic in smart cities—and aims help where it matters.  
**Peril.** If labels reflect access to care or policing, models can entrench bias; camera/EHR data raise privacy risks; unclear ownership weakens accountability.  
**Convergence & divergence.** Both deliver cloud-native ML with managed training and data platforms. They converge on “ML-as-a-service,” but diverge locally: **AWS** leads with modular tools and governance workflows; **Tencent** focuses on scenario bundles for cities/health/education and greener infrastructure.

**Ethics & SDGs.** To advance SDG **10/3/11**, pair explanations (model cards, SHAP) with purpose limits, privacy-by-design, audit logs, fairness checks across districts/hospitals, and publish the energy/water footprint of deployments.

### Part B — Liberal Arts & Global Leadership (how we do better together)
Start small and specific: **(1)** co-design a pilot with a local hospital or city bureau and agree on people-centric success metrics (wait times, equity, energy use); **(2)** evaluate **impact**, not just accuracy—use A/B tests or DiD around real policy changes; **(3)** publish a **governance card**: data sources, privacy protections, fairness/error metrics, plus PUE/WUE for compute.  
A liberal-arts lens keeps us critical of “tech fixes everything,” linking CS with economics (who benefits), public policy (rules), and ethics (rights/harms). The DKU joint-venture context helps translate **U.S. modular platforms** and **China’s scenario-first deployments** into **context-aware** ML that can advance SDGs (Climate Action, Quality Education, Decent Work).

## How this connects to my project
The same governance playbook applies to historical-text ML: document data provenance, calibrate sentiment thresholds with human audits, report uncertainty, and publish reproducible code. This improves credibility when testing whether the **1937** shock changed the tone of headlines using **“她.”**
